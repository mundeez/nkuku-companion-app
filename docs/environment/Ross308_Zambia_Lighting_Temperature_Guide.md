# Ross 308 Lighting & Temperature/Brooding Guide for Zambia

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Date:** 2026-07-09
**Target:** Commercial Ross 308 broiler production in Lusaka, Zambia and similar climatic zones in Southern Africa
**Evidence level:** Evidence-based with citations and confidence levels

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Ross 308 Overview

The Ross 308 is selected for a balanced combination of growth rate, feed conversion efficiency, meat yield, robustness, and adaptability to tropical environments. This guide focuses on two environmental levers that strongly influence early survival, flock uniformity, feed conversion, and welfare: **lighting** and **temperature/brooding**. Recommendations are taken primarily from the **Aviagen Ross Broiler Management Handbook 2025** and the **Ross Broiler Pocket Guide 2025**, with cross-checks against regional and extension sources.

### 1.2 Production Cycle in Zambia

A typical commercial broiler cycle in Lusaka runs from **day-old chick (DOC) placement to processing at 35–42 days**, depending on target market weight (usually 1.8–2.5 kg live weight). The Lusaka climate features:

- **Hot, wet season:** November–April (rainy season, high humidity).
- **Cool, dry season:** May–August (cold nights, low humidity).
- **Hot, dry season:** September–November (high temperatures, low humidity).

These seasonal variations directly affect in-house temperature, humidity, ventilation, and the feasibility of dark periods in open-sided housing. The guide therefore includes season-specific and housing-type adjustments.

### 1.3 Why Lighting and Temperature Matter

- **Lighting** controls feeding time, activity, rest, growth uniformity, leg health, and metabolic disorders such as ascites and sudden death.
- **Brooding temperature** drives early feed and water intake, gut development, immune competence, and 7-day body weight — the strongest predictor of final flock performance.
- Poor management of either factor increases mortality, culls, condemnations, and feed conversion ratio (FCR).

## 2. Lighting Programs

### 2.1 Primary Source: Aviagen Ross Broiler Management Handbook 2025

The 2025 handbook is the current authoritative breed management guide. It superseded the 2018 edition and is accompanied by the 2025 Pocket Guide and a 2025 Nutrition Supplement.

### 2.2 Day-Old Placement and Induction Period (Days 0–7)

Ross 308 chicks benefit from a long photoperiod immediately after placement so that every bird can locate feed and water quickly.

| Age (days) | Hours of light | Hours of dark | Light intensity (lux) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 | 23 | 1 | ≥ 30–40 (whole-house); 80–100 (spot brooding) | Encourages feed/water discovery; dark period < 0.4 lux |
| 0–7 | 23 initially; gradually reduce to 18–19 | 1 initially; gradually increase to 4–6 | ≥ 30–40 to day 7 | Step-down should reach 4–6 hours of continuous darkness by day 7 |

**Evidence:** Aviagen 2025 handbook recommends 23 hours light and 1 hour dark from placement, gradually reaching 4–6 hours darkness by 7 days. Intensity must be at least 30–40 lux in whole-house brooding or 80–100 lux at the edge of a spot brooder. During the dark period, intensity must remain below 0.4 lux.

### 2.3 Grow-Out Photoperiod (After Day 7)

Once chicks have learned where feed and water are located, a stable dark period supports welfare and performance.

| Age (days) | Hours of light | Hours of dark | Light intensity (lux) | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8–35/42 (grow-out) | 18–20 | 4–6 | 5–10 | One continuous block of darkness is preferred; supports rest, leg health, and metabolic function |
| Pre-catching (last 3 days) | 23 | 1 | 5–10 | Flushes feed from the gut and reduces transit time; also reduces catch stress |

**Evidence:** Aviagen 2025 Pocket Guide states: "Day 0–7: chicks should have 23 hours light and 1 hour dark from the first day, and gradually reduced to 4–6 hours of darkness by 7 days. After 7 days: dark period of 4–6 hours. Continuous or near continuous lighting is not optimal."

### 2.4 Light Intensity Targets by Phase

| Phase | Target intensity (lux) | Target intensity (foot-candles) | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brooding (0–7 days) | ≥ 30–40 (whole-house); 80–100 (spot brooder edge) | 2.8–3.7 fc (7.4–9.3 fc spot) | Stimulates activity, feed/water location, and early weight gain |
| Grow-out (8 days to depletion) | 5–10 | 0.46–0.93 fc | Sufficient for feeding and movement; minimizes flightiness and energy waste |
| Dark period | < 0.4 | < 0.04 fc | Allows true rest; prevents disruption of circadian rhythm |

**Key points:**
- Light must be uniformly distributed; variation between bright and dark areas should be < 30%.
- Use a light meter calibrated for the light source.
- Local welfare legislation may set higher minima (e.g., European minimum 20 lux at certain ages); always comply with national Zambian regulations where they exist.

### 2.5 Step-Down vs Step-Up Programs

- **Step-down (Aviagen 2025):** Start at 23L:1D and step down to 18–20L:4–6D by day 7. This is the default Ross 308 recommendation because it maximizes early intake while accustoming chicks to darkness.
- **Step-up (some integrator programs):** Begin with a shorter dark period (e.g., 22L:2D) and extend darkness later in the grow-out. Step-up programs are sometimes used to slow early growth and improve skeletal/frame development, but they are not the default Ross 308 guidance.

### 2.6 Ross 308-Specific Deviations from Generic Broiler Lighting

| Topic | Generic/extension guidance | Ross 308-specific guidance | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day 0–3 photoperiod | Many extension programs use 24L:0D (University of Georgia, 2007) | Aviagen 2025 recommends 23L:1D from day 0 | Ross gives a 1-hour dark induction rather than full continuous light |
| Day 4–7 dark period | Auburn example uses 18L:6D by days 3–4 | Aviagen says reach 4–6 hours darkness by day 7, preferring lights-on at the same time daily | Ross allows flexibility but emphasizes a single continuous dark block |
| Optimal daylength for growth | Older industry practice assumed 23L maximizes growth | Aviagen research (2010) found 20L:4D gave highest growth and feed intake in Ross 308/Ross 708 | Current 2025 handbook still uses 23L in week 1 for chick start, then 18–20L thereafter |
| Light intensity grow-out | Some generic guides drop to 5 lux at day 10 | Ross 2025 specifies 5–10 lux after 7 days | Ross upper bound helps maintain activity in open-sided houses |

### 2.7 Rationale and Welfare/Performance Trade-Offs

- **Early long photoperiod:** Chicks are poikilothermic for the first 4–5 days and cannot fully thermoregulate until 12–14 days. Maximizing feed/water intake in the first week is critical for immune development, yolk sac absorption, and 7-day body weight.
- **Dark period:** Providing 4–6 hours of continuous darkness improves:
  - Leg health and gait scores (reduced skeletal disorders).
  - Feed conversion efficiency (rest supports digestion and nutrient absorption).
  - Cardiovascular/respiratory health (reduced ascites and sudden death syndrome).
  - Welfare indicators such as footpad dermatitis and hock burn.
- **Ross 308 note:** A 2010 Aviagen/University of Saskatchewan study showed that **20 hours of light gave the highest body weight and feed intake** in Ross 308 and Ross 708, while 23 hours was inferior for growth, mortality, processing performance, and welfare.

## 3. Temperature and Brooding

### 3.1 Primary Source: Aviagen Ross Broiler Management Handbook 2025

The handbook gives target temperatures by body weight and by brooding system (whole-house vs spot). It also stresses that chick behavior and vent temperature are the ultimate guides.

### 3.3 Target Brooder Temperature by Age — Derived Schedule for Zambia

Using the Ross 2022 performance objectives to map age to body weight, the following day-by-week temperature schedule is derived from Table 2.4. It is suitable for whole-house or partial-house brooding in Zambian conditions.

| Age (days) | Approx. body weight (g) | Whole-house target (°C) | Spot-brooder edge target (°C) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (placement) | 40–45 | 30–32 | 32 | Preheat house 24 h; floor 28–30 °C; litter 28–32 °C |
| 1–3 | 50–80 | 30 | 30–32 | RH 60–70%; watch vent temp 39.4–40.5 °C |
| 4–7 | 100–180 | 28–29 | 29–30 | Begin transition to automated feeders; expand brooding area |
| 8–14 | 290–560 | 26–27 | 27–28 | RH can drop toward 50%; brooding rings removed in closed houses by 7 days, open-sided by 10–12 days |
| 15–21 | 600–1,050 | 24–25 | 25–26 | Transition to finisher feed around day 25 |
| 22–28 | 1,100–1,600 | 22–23 | 23–24 | Birds produce substantial metabolic heat; avoid over-heating |

| 36 to depletion | >2,300 | 20 (or ambient if below 20 and birds comfortable) | 20 | Maintain air quality; pre-catch temperature 23L:1D |

### 3.4 Relative Humidity Targets

| Phase | Target RH | Consequences outside target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Placement to 3 days | 60–70% | < 50% can dehydrate chicks and increase dust; > 70% favors pathogens and wet litter |
| After 3 days to depletion | 50–60% | > 70% at > 29 °C impairs performance; < 50% increases dust and respiratory stress |

**Evidence:** Ross 2025 handbook states that Table 2.4 temperatures are based on RH 60–70% up to 3 days and 50% after that. The pocket guide includes an RH correction table showing how dry-bulb temperature should be adjusted at different RH levels, especially for chicks < 200 g.

### 3.5 Chick Behavior and Vent Temperature as the Final Guides

Temperature and RH should be monitored daily, but **bird behavior is the best indicator**:

| Observation | Interpretation | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chicks evenly spread, content vocalization, active feeding/drinking | Temperature correct | Maintain |
| Chicks huddle under brooder, loud distress calls | Too cold | Increase heat or reduce drafts |
| Chicks move away from heat, pant, drooping wings/heads, quiet | Too hot | Reduce temperature or increase ventilation |
| Uneven distribution not related to heat source | Draft, light leak, noise, or poor feeder/drinker placement | Investigate environment |

**Vent temperature target for the first 2 days:** 39.4–40.5 °C (103–105 °F). This is more reliable than air temperature alone during the first 48 hours.

### 3.6 Ross 308-Specific Brooding Guidance vs Generic

| Topic | Generic/extension guidance | Ross 2025 guidance | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Placement air temperature | WRC South Africa: 32 °C for first week | Ross: 30 °C whole-house, 32 °C spot-brooder edge | WRC figure is closer to spot-brooding edge; Ross distinguishes system type |
| Temperature step-down by age | WRC: 32 °C days 1–7, 29 °C days 8–14, 27 °C days 15–21, 24 °C days 22–28, 21 °C day 29+ | Ross gives weight-based table; equivalent age schedule is similar but front-loaded differently | WRC simplifies to weekly blocks; Ross uses weight/behavior for precision |
| Humidity emphasis | Many generic guides mention 50–70% RH | Ross: 60–70% up to day 3, then 50%; provides explicit RH correction table | Ross more precise for small chicks where RH has largest impact |
| Water temperature | Generic: cool, fresh | Ross: 18–21 °C ideal | Ross specifies range to avoid reduced intake |
| Preheating duration | Generic: 24 h minimum | Ross: at least 24 h; longer in cold/new houses | Ross emphasizes structural mass must be warm, not just air |

### 3.7 Regional Adjustment Notes for Zambia

#### Housing types in Zambia

| Housing type | Typical control | Lighting/temperature implications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open-sided/naturally ventilated | Curtains, roof insulation, circulation fans | Daylight leaks make true dark periods hard; ambient temperature follows outdoor conditions; highest risk in hot dry season |
| Closed-environment (power ventilated) | Tunnel/minimum ventilation, evaporative cooling, timers/dimmers | Allows precise light/dark and temperature control; requires backup generator |
| Mixed/part-house brooding | Curtains plus localized heaters | Spot or partial-house brooding common in small-to-medium operations; temperature gradient as per Table 2.4 |

#### Seasonal adjustments for Lusaka

| Season | Risk | Temperature management | Lighting management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hot, wet (Nov–Apr) | Heat + high humidity; wet litter; ammonia | Use tunnel/circulation fans; flush water lines to keep water cool (< 21 °C); lower stocking density if open-sided | Dark period may coincide with hottest afternoon if artificial light is shifted to cooler early morning/night hours |
| Cool, dry (May–Aug) | Cold nights, low humidity, drafts | Preheat longer; monitor floor temp; avoid sudden curtain openings dropping cold air onto chicks | Maintain minimum light intensity because low-angle sunlight is weaker; ensure timers do not leave birds in darkness during coldest pre-dawn hours |
| Hot, dry (Sep–Nov) | Extreme heat, low humidity, dust | Evaporative cooling if available; roof sprinkling; increase ventilation; avoid over-heating | Provide artificial light during cooler hours; protect from bright direct sunlight entering open sides |

#### Open-sided house considerations
- Brooding rings may need to stay in place until **10–12 days** in open-sided houses (vs 7 days in closed houses).
- Natural daylight makes a true 0.4-lux dark period difficult. Use blackout curtains where possible, and schedule the artificial dark period during the coolest or hottest part of the day to reduce heat stress.
- In open-sided houses, the period of artificial light should be timed to **maximize bird comfort**, not simply clock time.

#### Altitude note
Lusaka sits at approximately 1,200–1,300 m above sea level. The lower air density slightly reduces convective and evaporative cooling compared with sea level; therefore, heat stress mitigation is slightly more important than the raw temperature suggests.

## 4. Critical Control Points and Alerts

### 4.1 Daily Checks

| Item | Frequency | Target/Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air temperature at chick height | At least twice daily in first 5 days, then daily | Match age/body-weight schedule; adjust by behavior |
| Litter/floor temperature | Before placement and daily in first week | 28–32 °C at placement |
| Relative humidity | Daily | 60–70% first 3 days; 50–60% thereafter |
| Light intensity | At placement and weekly | ≥ 30–40 lux brooding; 5–10 lux grow-out; < 0.4 lux dark period |
| Vent temperature | First 2 days | 39.4–40.5 °C |
| Chick behavior/crop fill | First 24 h and ongoing | > 95% full crops by 24 h; even distribution |
| Timer/backup power | Daily | Ensure lights return on schedule; test generator |

### 4.2 Common Failures to Flag

- **24-hour continuous light beyond day 1–2:** not optimal for Ross 308 welfare or final performance.
- **Dark period split into short intermittent flashes:** must include at least one continuous 4-hour block.
- **Over-reliance on air temperature:** floor/litter temperature and chick vent temperature are equally important.
- **Ignoring RH:** high humidity magnifies heat stress; low humidity increases dust and dehydration.
- **Sudden large changes in temperature or light:** always make adjustments gradually (over 2–3 days for light; 1–2 °C steps for temperature).

## 7. Conflicts and Flags for Implementation

1. **Ross 2025 still uses 23L in week 1, while the 2010 Aviagen research says 23L is not optimal for grow-out.** The conflict is resolved by context: 23L is for *chick start only* (days 0–7), after which 18–20L is preferred.
2. **WRC small-scale guide recommends 32 °C for the first week, while Ross 2025 recommends 30 °C for whole-house brooding.** The difference reflects housing type: 32 °C aligns with spot-brooder edge temperature, not whole-house air temperature.
3. **Open-sided houses cannot easily achieve < 0.4 lux dark periods.** In Zambia, expect to approximate the dark period with blackout curtains and adjust timing to ambient temperature.
4. **Local legislation may override technical recommendations.** Always check Zambian animal welfare and food-safety regulations for light, dark, feed withdrawal, and catching practices.
